Trade and battle with Pokémon FireRed and LeafGreen on the Nintendo Switch: from a real Game Boy Advance, from this page, or online with Celio. This page also sends it Mystery Gift Wonder Cards.

ESP32 board

Joins the Switch's wireless. ESP32-S3, C6, C5, C3 or the original ESP32.

The Switch's wireless is encrypted with keys from the console. Four of them go from this file to the board over USB. Nothing is uploaded.

Play

Let this page carry the link, or connect the two boards with three wires.

Ruby and Sapphire have no wireless. This page links them by cable and turns that link into the Switch's wireless, so both boards stay on USB. Trades and single or double battles, with the Switch leading.

Trades go ahead whatever either game's progress. Pokémon from Ruby, Sapphire or Emerald may not be tradable out of the Switch's game until it has the National Dex and has finished the Sevii Islands story (Cerulean Cave shows on its town map). Only while this page carries the link, not with the boards wired. FireRed and LeafGreen need no bypass: leave it off for them.

On the consoles
  1. Switch: upstairs in a Pokémon Center, Direct Corner, Trade Center or Colosseum (single or double battle), become the leader.
  2. Ruby or Sapphire, with the adapter on the link cable: upstairs in a Pokémon Center, talk to the attendant at the middle counter to trade, or the left one for the same kind of battle.
  3. The page joins the Switch's group once the Game Boy Advance has linked. Accept the join on the Switch, then walk to the trade table, or onto the battle spots, on both consoles.
  4. Leave the room on both consoles when you are done. After that the board restarts and is ready again about ten seconds later.
  1. Switch: upstairs in a Pokémon Center, Direct Corner, Trade Center or Colosseum, become the leader.
  2. Game Boy Advance, with the adapter on a Game Boy Color cable: pick the same thing and join the group. The Switch shows up after a few seconds.
  3. Or the other way round: become the leader on the Game Boy Advance and join its group on the Switch.
  4. After you leave the room, the board restarts and is ready again about ten seconds later.

Trade

Trade with the Switch from this page. The board from step 1 is all it takes.

The pool picks the Pokémon you get, and what the Switch gives goes into the pool for the next person. See what is in it at pokemon.gblink.io/pool.

Your own party as .pk3 files, kept in this browser. What the Switch sends takes the place of what you gave.

The Switch

The pool

Once you are both at the trade table, click a Pokémon to offer it. ↓ saves one as a .pk3 file, ↑ replaces it; dropping a file on a slot does the same.

The pool's Pokémon shows up here once you are both at the trade table. Accept trade offers it, before or after you pick yours on the Switch. Cancel trade declines it. For a different one, leave the table and sit down again.

Kept here

The pool did not confirm these trades, so what the Switch gave is kept here. ↓ saves one as a .pk3 file.

On the Switch
  1. Upstairs in a Pokémon Center, Direct Corner, Trade Center, become the leader.
  2. Press Connect here, then accept the join on the Switch.
  3. Sit down at the trade table.
  4. CANCEL at the table takes you back to the room; sit down again for another trade. Leaving the room ends the visit.
More options

Keep the tab in view while you trade: a hidden tab runs too slowly for the game.

A Pokémon that arrives takes the slot of the one that left. Offer it again to trade it straight back.

How it works
  1. Create a session and send its Session Id to the other player, or join theirs. On a Game Boy Advance with a GB-Link, or in an emulator, they use celio.gblink.io. Both press Start.
  2. The Celio server decides which side leads. The page then says whether to lead a group on the Switch or to join the group it opens, and in that case asks which room.
  3. On the Switch: upstairs in a Pokémon Center, the Direct Corner, then the Trade Center or the Colosseum. The other player goes to the same room.
  4. Leaving the room on both consoles ends the session. Keep this tab in view while you play.
Options: National Dex bypass, Celio server

Trades go ahead whatever either game's progress. Pokémon from Ruby, Sapphire or Emerald may not be tradable out of the Switch's game until it has the National Dex and has finished the Sevii Islands story (Cerulean Cave shows on its town map).

Mystery Gift

Send Wonder Cards to the Switch from this page. The board from step 1 is all it takes.

Your own Wonder Card (.wc3 file)

–

Backed-up saves

    On the Switch
    1. Mystery Gift has to be on the game's main menu first: answer a Poké Mart questionnaire with LINK TOGETHER WITH ALL, then save. The first Poké Mart shows its questionnaire once you have the Pokédex.
    2. Press Start here, then on the Switch pick MYSTERY GIFT, WONDER CARDS, FRIEND, and choose GBLINK.
    3. Once the card is saved, talk to the deliveryman in green upstairs in any Pokémon Center.
    4. The page stays ready: pick another card for the next one, on this Switch or another.
    Where the cards come from

    All of them come from gblink-wondercards. Event distributions and the Project Wonder (Goppier) events are the FireRed and LeafGreen events there, the same bytes the distribution cartridges send. The GB-Link Team's cards are built for the Switch's FireRed and LeafGreen and run on its English games.

    From the Switch

      On the Switch
      1. In Tomodachi Life, open Local Play and press Look for another player, then press Start here. The page joins as the other player.
      2. To keep a Mii, send it from the Switch: the page accepts it and lists it under From the Switch. Download it to send it again later.
      3. To send one, drop it on this card, change its name, voice, personality or other details if you like, and press Send to the Switch. The island takes it as a new resident; a full island declines it.
      4. Custom food and clothing the Switch sent go back the same way.
      About these files

      They are exactly what the game sends over Local Play. Each send gets new transfer IDs, and a Mii gets a new Mii ID, so an island takes a Mii again even if it came from there. The page keeps what the Switch sent only while it is open. Needs the board's firmware 2.2.0 or later.
